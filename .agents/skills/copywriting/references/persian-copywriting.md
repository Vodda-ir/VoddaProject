# Persian / Farsi Conversion Copywriting Rules

Use this reference whenever the requested copy is primarily Persian (Farsi), bilingual Persian/English, or aimed at Persian-speaking users.

## Goal

Write Persian that sounds like a competent Iranian product copywriter wrote it for the specific product and audience. Optimize for clarity, credibility, specificity, and conversion. Do not optimize for AI-detector scores or try to imitate mistakes merely to look human.

## Default register

- For modern SaaS, apps, dashboards, and SMB products: use natural contemporary Iranian Persian.
- Prefer conversational-professional Persian over bureaucratic, literary, or translated-from-English prose.
- Use respectful singular/plural consistently based on product voice. Do not randomly switch between «تو» and «شما».
- If no voice is specified, prefer direct professional «شما» for public marketing pages and concise neutral imperative labels inside product UI.
- Keep English technical terms only when the audience genuinely uses them. Otherwise use the common Persian term.

## Persian copy hierarchy

1. Say what the product does.
2. Say who it is for when that matters.
3. Name the concrete outcome or pain removed.
4. Add proof only when proof actually exists.
5. Add personality only after the meaning is already clear.

## Anti-generic rules

Avoid generic Persian marketing language unless it is literally the user's established brand voice:

- «راهکاری جامع»
- «راهکاری هوشمند»
- «راهکاری نوین»
- «تجربه‌ای متفاوت»
- «تحولی نوین»
- «گامی نوین»
- «دنیای امروز» / «دنیای پرشتاب امروز»
- «در عصر دیجیتال»
- «کسب‌وکار خود را به سطح بعدی ببرید»
- «قدرت را در دستان شما قرار می‌دهد»
- «همراه شما در مسیر موفقیت»
- «همه‌چیز در یک نگاه» when it does not describe a real information architecture
- «ساده، سریع و هوشمند» and other unsupported adjective triplets
- «یکپارچه، قدرتمند و انعطاف‌پذیر»
- «بهینه‌سازی فرآیندها» when the actual process can be named
- «افزایش بهره‌وری» without saying how
- «مدیریت هوشمند» without a concrete mechanism
- «بهترین انتخاب برای...» without evidence

Replace abstract claims with a concrete user action or result.

### Example

Weak:
> هما، راهکاری جامع و هوشمند برای مدیریت مالی کسب‌وکار شما.

Better:
> فروش، هزینه‌ها، حساب‌ها و فاکتورها را از یک جا مدیریت کنید.

Weak:
> کسب‌وکار خود را به سطح جدیدی ببرید.

Better:
> فاکتور بزنید، مانده حساب‌ها را ببینید و دخل‌وخرج روزانه را مرتب نگه دارید.

## Headlines

A Persian headline should usually do at least one of these:

- name the outcome,
- name the problem removed,
- explain the category clearly,
- differentiate with a concrete mechanism,
- identify the intended audience.

Avoid headlines that are only mood, aspiration, or adjectives.

Prefer:
> حسابداری روزمره کسب‌وکارتان، بدون دفتر و فایل اکسل

Over:
> آینده مدیریت مالی، همین امروز

Do not force rhyme, parallelism, or three-part slogans. A short plain sentence is often stronger.

## Subheads

Use the subhead to explain the headline, not to restate it with synonyms.

Bad pair:
> حسابداری ساده‌تر از همیشه
>
> تجربه‌ای ساده و هوشمند برای مدیریت بهتر امور مالی

Better pair:
> حسابداری روزمره کسب‌وکارتان، بدون دفتر و فایل اکسل
>
> فاکتور، دریافت‌وپرداخت، موجودی و مانده حساب مشتری‌ها را در یک محیط نگه دارید.

## CTAs

CTA text must reveal the next action. Prefer verbs and concrete outcomes.

Good:
- «شروع رایگان»
- «ساخت کسب‌وکار»
- «دیدن نسخه نمایشی»
- «ثبت اولین فاکتور»
- «افزودن مشتری»
- «مشاهده قیمت‌ها»

Use generic labels such as «ادامه»، «بیشتر»، «ثبت»، or «کلیک کنید» only when the surrounding context makes the consequence unambiguous.

## Persian rhythm

- Vary sentence length naturally.
- Do not make every section headline follow the same grammatical pattern.
- Do not make every feature card exactly two symmetric sentences.
- Avoid repetitive openings such as «با [محصول]...» on every section.
- Do not overuse rhetorical questions.
- Do not manufacture colloquial spelling or typos to appear human.
- Read the copy as spoken Iranian Persian. If it sounds like a translated brochure, rewrite it.

## English-to-Persian translation traps

Do not translate English marketing formulas mechanically.

Examples:

- "Unlock the power of X" → do not write «قدرت X را آزاد کنید». State the actual benefit.
- "Seamless experience" → do not default to «تجربه‌ای یکپارچه و بی‌نقص». Explain what no longer interrupts the user.
- "Take control of your business" → avoid «کنترل کسب‌وکار خود را به دست بگیرید» unless control is genuinely the point.
- "All-in-one" → use only if the product really replaces multiple named tools; otherwise list the relevant jobs.
- "Built for you" → name the actual audience.
- "Effortlessly" → do not promise «بدون زحمت» unless the workflow supports the claim.

## Claims and proof

Never invent:
- customer counts,
- percentages,
- time savings,
- rankings,
- market leadership,
- testimonials,
- security/compliance claims,
- integrations,
- feature availability.

When evidence is absent, use a concrete product description instead of fake proof.

## B2B / business software

For Iranian business software, buyers usually care about practical questions. When relevant, address real issues such as:

- What work becomes easier?
- What replaces manual work or spreadsheets?
- How long does setup take, if known?
- Is data export/backup available, if known?
- Does the product support multiple users/branches, if known?
- What happens to current records/imports, if known?
- Is there a demo/trial, if known?

Do not imply features that are not confirmed in product context.

## Typography and orthography

- Use Persian characters «ی» and «ک», not Arabic ي/ك.
- Use Persian punctuation and spacing naturally.
- Use نیم‌فاصله where it improves normal Persian spelling: «می‌توانید»، «کسب‌وکار»، «همه‌چیز».
- Do not over-correct colloquial product terminology if the audience commonly writes it another way.
- Keep numerals consistent with the product locale/design system.
- Avoid excessive quotation marks, ellipses, exclamation marks, and decorative punctuation.

## Final Persian copy audit

Before returning copy, check:

1. Could this headline belong to ten unrelated SaaS products? If yes, make it more specific.
2. Does every adjective earn its place with a mechanism or proof?
3. Did English syntax leak into the Persian sentence?
4. Can a visitor understand what the product does within the first screen?
5. Are CTA consequences clear?
6. Are there unsupported superlatives or metrics?
7. Are feature headings varied rather than mechanically parallel?
8. Does the text sound natural when read aloud by an Iranian speaker?
9. Can any sentence be shorter without losing useful meaning?
10. Is the copy clear enough before any branding flourish is added?

If a `humanizer` skill is installed, run it as the final editing pass after the copy is factually and strategically correct.
